# NOVA CITY World Bible

## World rule

In 2097, mobility is economic power. Vehicles are transport, livelihood, status, evidence, community identity, and sometimes weapons. Roads, charging/fuel infrastructure, telemetry, and vehicle networks are strategic infrastructure.

## Launch districts

Machine-readable definitions live in `design/catalog/districts.json`.

### Central Arcology
Civic towers, transit exchanges, public cameras, official events, and strong NOVA Authority presence.

### Glass Crown
Corporate skyline and luxury economy. VANTEX influence is strongest here; high-value contracts and invitation-only venues dominate.

### Foundry 9
Industrial fabrication, scrapyards, and Mechanist workshops. Primary home of build progression, salvage, and Garage 17.

### Docklands
Freight, containers, shipyards, labor networks, convoy routes, and contraband stories.

### Neon Mile
Nightlife, car meets, clubs, street culture, and public-facing racing celebrity.

### Dust Verge
Outer desert, abandoned energy infrastructure, long off-road routes, and Free Roads activity.

### Greenbelt
Reclaimed ecological zone, reservoirs, protected roads, exploration, and pet/scout utility.

### Old Grid
Pre-war streets, decaying network infrastructure, archives, and hidden DRIVE ZERO history.

### Skyway
Elevated expressways, vertical interchanges, time attack, sanctioned racing, and Grand Prix infrastructure.

### Black Circuit
A shifting underground race network rather than a legally recognized district. Access depends on reputation and introductions.

## Persistent world-state scopes

- **Global season state:** shared events, championship phases, weather/event rotation.
- **Shard/community state:** local crew/faction signals when compatible with fairness.
- **Character state:** NPC trust, story aftermath, personal access, private instances, selected ambience.

This layering prevents one player's ending from invalidating another player's world.

## Tone

NOVA CITY should be seductive before it is dystopian: speed, beauty, invention, nightlife, work, and belonging sit above the question of who controls the infrastructure underneath.
